# Skin Concerns Overview

## Overview

Skin concerns are specific issues a person wants to address, distinct from their underlying skin type. A person can have oily skin and still experience dehydration. Someone with dry skin can develop acne. Identifying the correct concern leads to the appropriate treatment actives.

## 1. Acne

Acne vulgaris is a chronic inflammatory condition of the pilosebaceous unit (hair follicle + sebaceous gland). It is the most common skin condition globally, affecting approximately 85% of people at some point.

### Types of Acne Lesions

| Type | Description | Treatment Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Comedonal (blackheads/whiteheads) | Non-inflammatory; plugged follicles | BHA, retinol, AHA |
| Inflammatory (papules/pustules) | Red, raised, infected lesions | BPO, BHA, niacinamide, azelaic acid |
| Cystic/nodular | Deep, painful, severe | Prescription retinoids, antibiotics, isotretinoin |

### Key Active Ingredients for Acne

- **Salicylic acid (BHA):** unclogs pores, anti-inflammatory; ideal for comedonal and mild inflammatory acne.
- **Benzoyl peroxide:** kills C. acnes; best for inflammatory acne.
- **Retinol/tretinoin:** normalises follicular keratinisation; effective for all types.
- **Niacinamide:** anti-inflammatory, sebum control; supportive ingredient.
- **Azelaic acid:** antimicrobial + anti-inflammatory; dual-action for mixed acne types.
- **Adapalene (Differin 0.1%):** prescription-strength retinoid now OTC in many markets; highly effective.

### Acne Triggers (Common)
- Hormonal fluctuations (androgens increase sebum production).
- Diet (high-glycaemic foods; dairy, in some individuals).
- Comedogenic products (check INCI lists; avoid heavy oils, coconut oil on acne-prone skin).
- Physical friction (helmet straps, phone contact, touching face).
- Stress (cortisol triggers sebum production).

## 2. Hyperpigmentation

Hyperpigmentation refers to darkening of skin patches caused by excess melanin deposition. It is not a single condition — the type determines the most effective treatment.

### Types

- **Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH):** dark spots left after acne, injuries, or inflammation. More prominent in darker skin tones (Fitzpatrick III–VI). Treatment: azelaic acid, vitamin C, niacinamide, AHA, retinol.
- **Melasma:** chronic, hormonally-driven hyperpigmentation (estrogen, progesterone, UV). Symmetrical distribution on cheeks, upper lip, forehead. Resistant to treatment; SPF is mandatory. Treatment: azelaic acid 15–20%, hydroquinone (Rx), tranexamic acid, kojic acid.
- **Solar lentigines (sun spots, age spots):** UV-induced focal melanin deposits. Treatment: AHA, retinol, vitamin C, laser (dermatologist).
- **Freckles (ephelides):** genetic; darken with UV exposure. Reduce with SPF and vitamin C; temporary.

### Essential Rule for All Hyperpigmentation
**SPF is non-negotiable.** UV exposure triggers tyrosinase and worsens all forms of hyperpigmentation. Without SPF, any brightening active is working against active UV damage simultaneously.

## 3. Ageing (Photoageing and Intrinsic Ageing)

Skin ageing has two components:
- **Intrinsic ageing:** genetically programmed; slows collagen and elastin synthesis; occurs regardless of sun exposure.
- **Photoageing:** UV-driven; responsible for 80–90% of visible skin ageing; includes fine lines, wrinkles, rough texture, uneven tone, and hyperpigmentation.

### Key Actives for Anti-Ageing
- **Retinol/tretinoin:** gold standard; stimulates collagen, accelerates cell turnover, reduces fine lines.
- **Peptides (Matrixyl, GHK-Cu):** signal fibroblasts to produce collagen; well-tolerated alternative or complement to retinol.
- **Vitamin C:** prevents UV-induced collagen degradation; essential with SPF.
- **SPF:** prevents 80–90% of photoageing — the most impactful single intervention.
- **AHA (glycolic acid):** long-term use stimulates collagen at dermis level; improves texture and tone.
- **Niacinamide:** reduces fine lines at higher concentrations (5%+); barrier support.

## 4. Redness and Rosacea

Rosacea is a chronic inflammatory condition characterised by facial flushing, persistent redness (erythema), visible blood vessels (telangiectasia), and inflammatory papules/pustules. Common triggers: heat, spicy food, alcohol, UV exposure, stress.

### Key Actives for Rosacea and Redness
- **Azelaic acid 15%:** reduces papules, pustules, and redness; inhibits kallikrein-5 pathway. Gold standard for rosacea.
- **Niacinamide (4–5%):** reduces redness via anti-inflammatory pathway.
- **Centella Asiatica (Cica):** soothing; supports barrier; reduces redness.
- **Avoid:** fragrance, alcohol, strong AHAs, high-concentration vitamin C (low pH stings), physical scrubs.
- **SPF is essential** — UV is a major rosacea trigger.

## 5. Dehydration vs Dryness

Often confused; require different solutions.

| | Dehydration | Dryness |
|---|---|---|
| What is lacking | Water | Oil / lipids |
| Can affect | Any skin type (including oily) | Dry skin type specifically |
| Visual signs | Tight skin, dull, fine lines exaggerated | Flaking, scaling, rough texture |
| Treatment | Humectants (hyaluronic acid, glycerin) | Emollients + occlusives (ceramides, shea, squalane) |
