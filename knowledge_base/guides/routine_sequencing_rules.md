# Routine Sequencing Rules

## Overview

The order in which skincare products are applied is not arbitrary. It affects ingredient delivery, efficacy, and the risk of interactions. The core principles are: thinnest to thickest consistency, pH considerations for actives, and SPF always last in the morning.

## The Thin-to-Thick Principle

Water-based products with thin consistency (toners, essence, serums) must be applied before thicker, oil-based, or occlusive products (moisturisers, face oils, sunscreen). If a thick, occlusive product is applied first, it creates a physical barrier that prevents thinner, water-based actives from penetrating the skin.

**Why:** skin absorption follows a gradient — low molecular weight, water-based molecules penetrate the stratum corneum more readily. Thick creams and oils seal the surface, blocking subsequent absorption.

## Canonical AM Routine Order

1. **Cleanser** — remove overnight sebum, sweat, and product residue.
2. **Toner / hydrating essence** (optional) — restore skin pH post-cleansing; add first layer of hydration.
3. **Vitamin C serum** (if used) — antioxidant protection works best before SPF; requires acidic pH.
4. **Serum** — targeted active ingredients (niacinamide, peptides, hyaluronic acid, AHA if used AM).
5. **Eye cream** (optional) — applied before moisturiser.
6. **Moisturiser** — seals in serums, provides barrier support.
7. **SPF** — always the final step; applying anything over SPF disrupts the filter film.

## Canonical PM Routine Order

1. **Oil cleanser or micellar water** (if wearing SPF or makeup) — first cleanse to dissolve lipid-based products.
2. **Water-based cleanser** — second cleanse to remove remaining residue.
3. **Toner / essence** (optional).
4. **Exfoliant** (AHA or BHA, 2–3× per week) — applied to clean skin; needs direct contact with stratum corneum for efficacy.
5. **Wait 10–20 minutes** — allows acid to work at optimal pH before it is neutralised by subsequent products.
6. **Treatment serum** (retinol, prescription retinoid, niacinamide, peptides) — thin, active layer.
7. **Eye cream** (optional).
8. **Moisturiser** — locks in actives; ceramide-rich formulas help counteract retinol/acid barrier disruption.
9. **Face oil** (if used) — applies last as it creates an occlusive seal over water-based layers.

## pH Considerations

Some actives require a specific environmental pH to function. Applying alkaline products (most moisturisers, pH ~5.5–7) immediately after an acidic active (pH 3–4) neutralises the acid before it has finished working.

| Active | Optimal pH | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| L-Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C) | <3.5 | Apply first among actives; allow to absorb ~2 min |
| AHA (glycolic, lactic acid) | 3–4 | Apply to bare skin; wait 10–20 min before next product |
| BHA (salicylic acid) | 3–4 | Same as AHA |
| Retinol | ~5.5 neutral | Apply after any low-pH actives have absorbed |
| Niacinamide | Flexible (3–9) | Apply after actives; no waiting needed |
| Hyaluronic Acid | Flexible | Apply after actives, on slightly damp skin |

## Wait Times

Mandatory wait times are often debated. The practical guidance:
- **After acids (AHA/BHA):** wait 10–20 minutes before applying the next product for optimal exfoliation time.
- **After retinol (beginners):** wait 10–15 minutes before moisturiser (reduces irritation via "buffering").
- **Vitamin C:** 1–2 minutes is sufficient; longer not necessary for most formulations.
- **Between all other steps:** immediate layering is fine.

## Oils After Water-Based Products

If using a face oil (squalane, rosehip, jojoba), it must go after all water-based serums and moisturisers. Oil acts as an occlusive seal. Applying it earlier will prevent absorption of water-based products applied on top.

**Exception:** some cleansing oils and balms are designed for the first-cleanse step.

## SPF Placement

SPF is always the **last step** in the AM routine. SPF filters form a film on the skin surface. Applying other products on top of SPF:
1. Physically disrupts the filter film.
2. Can alter the pH, stability, or distribution of both the SPF filter and the active.

If additional coverage (tinted moisturiser, foundation) is needed, apply on top of SPF but be aware that this does not add meaningful UV protection.

## Common Sequencing Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Applying vitamin C after moisturiser | L-AA cannot penetrate through occlusive layer |
| Applying SPF before moisturiser | Products applied over SPF disrupt filter film |
| Layering AHA immediately with retinol at same pH | Severe irritation, barrier damage |
| Applying hyaluronic acid as the final step in dry conditions | Can dehydrate skin by drawing moisture outward |
