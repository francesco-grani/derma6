# Skin Type Classification Guide

## Overview

Accurate skin type identification is the foundation of any effective skincare routine. Using products formulated for the wrong skin type can worsen concerns — heavy creams on oily skin can clog pores; harsh cleansers on dry skin worsen barrier disruption. Skin type is largely genetic but can be influenced by age, climate, hormones, medications, and season.

**Important distinction:** skin type refers to intrinsic characteristics of the skin (oil production, barrier function). Skin condition refers to temporary states that can affect any skin type (dehydration, sensitivity reactions, breakouts).

## The Five Primary Skin Types

### 1. Oily Skin

**Characteristics:**
- Visible shine across the entire face, especially prominent in the T-zone (forehead, nose, chin) within 1–2 hours of cleansing.
- Enlarged, visible pores, particularly on the nose and cheeks.
- Prone to blackheads, whiteheads, and inflammatory acne.
- Makeup slides or "breaks down" through the day.

**Cause:** overactive sebaceous glands producing excess sebum. Driven by androgens (testosterone, DHT) — this is why oily skin is particularly common in males and during puberty.

**Diagnostic test:** blot a tissue on the face 1–2 hours after cleansing (no moisturiser). Oil on the tissue across the entire face indicates oily skin.

**Recommended approach:** lightweight, non-comedogenic, oil-free moisturisers; gel-based cleansers; BHA (salicylic acid) for pore management; niacinamide for sebum control.

### 2. Dry Skin

**Characteristics:**
- Tight, uncomfortable sensation after cleansing.
- Flaking, scaling, or rough texture, especially on cheeks.
- Dull, lacklustre appearance.
- Fine lines appear more pronounced due to lack of moisture.
- Rarely experiences breakouts.

**Cause:** reduced sebum production and/or compromised skin barrier leading to elevated trans-epidermal water loss (TEWL). Can worsen with age, cold weather, and excessive cleansing.

**Diagnostic test:** face feels tight and appears dull with visible dry patches 1–2 hours after cleansing.

**Recommended approach:** cream or oil-based moisturisers with ceramides, hyaluronic acid, and occlusives (shea butter, squalane); gentle, non-foaming cleansers; avoid harsh actives until barrier is restored.

### 3. Combination Skin

**Characteristics:**
- Oily or shiny T-zone (forehead, nose, chin).
- Normal to dry cheeks.
- Pores visible primarily on and around the nose.
- May experience both blackheads (T-zone) and dry patches (cheeks) simultaneously.

**Cause:** uneven distribution of sebaceous glands — higher density in the T-zone — combined with potentially different environmental factors affecting different facial zones.

**Recommended approach:** zone-specific care (BHA on T-zone, richer moisturiser on cheeks); gel-cream hybrid moisturisers; niacinamide works well for balancing both zones.

### 4. Sensitive Skin

**Characteristics:**
- Frequent redness, stinging, burning, or itching in response to products.
- Reactive to environmental triggers (heat, cold, wind, spicy food).
- Skin flushes easily.
- Prone to rashes or reactions with fragranced or alcohol-containing products.

**Note:** sensitive skin is a condition that can co-exist with any skin type. It is not an independent category in all classification systems. It overlaps with rosacea-prone skin.

**Recommended approach:** fragrance-free, alcohol-free, minimal-ingredient products; patch-test new products; avoid multiple active ingredients simultaneously; prioritise barrier repair (ceramides, niacinamide).

### 5. Normal Skin

**Characteristics:**
- Balanced sebum production — not visibly oily, not dry.
- Small, barely visible pores.
- Smooth texture, even tone.
- Tolerates most skincare products without reaction.
- Rarely breaks out.

**Note:** "normal" skin is relatively uncommon; most people lean toward combination, oily, or dry.

## Dehydrated Skin (Condition, Not Type)

Dehydrated skin lacks water — not oil. It can affect any skin type, including oily skin. Key distinguishing signs:
- Skin feels tight but still looks oily or has normal sebum levels.
- Fine lines appear exaggerated when you gently pinch and release facial skin.
- Dull appearance despite adequate oil production.

**Cause:** environmental exposure (dry air, heating/air conditioning), excessive alcohol consumption, insufficient water intake, over-cleansing, or barrier disruption.

**Treatment:** hyaluronic acid serums, lightweight hydrating toners, reducing cleansing frequency.

## Acneic Skin (Condition)

Acne-prone skin is a condition that can affect oily, combination, or even dry skin types. Characterised by a tendency to develop comedones, papules, pustules, or cysts. See Skin Concerns Overview for treatment.
