# Ingredient Irritancy Reference

## Overview

This document provides an irritancy profile for each of the 11 core actives in the Knowledge Base, plus the two SPF actives. It is sourced from INCI Decoder ingredient pages (incidecoder.com) and validated against community data from r/SkincareAddiction. It is intended to support the Introduction Scheduler Tool and the Routine Sequencer when flagging high-irritancy pairings.

**Important distinction from the Conflict Table:** This document covers standalone irritancy (how irritating an ingredient is on its own, to skin in general). The Conflict Table covers paired interactions (what happens when two ingredients are used together). Both are needed for a complete safety picture.

## Per-Ingredient Irritancy Notes

### Retinol
*INCI Decoder classification: cell-communicating ingredient / superstar*

Retinol is "10-to-20 times less potent than retinoic acid" (INCI Decoder) and has "much less" irritation than prescription tretinoin — but it is still the highest-irritancy ingredient in this KB's scope. Irritation manifests as redness, peeling, dryness, and temporary tightness (retinoid dermatitis). Symptoms typically peak in weeks 2–4 and resolve by weeks 6–8 as skin adjusts. Pregnant users must not use any retinoid.

**Photosensitivity:** High — PM use only; mandatory SPF every morning.
**Management:** buffer over moisturiser, start at 2× per week, increase slowly.

---

### Glycolic Acid (AHA)
*INCI Decoder classification: exfoliant, buffering / superstar*

"Slight stinging or burning with a stronger AHA product is normal" (INCI Decoder). Concentration and pH both drive irritation — effectiveness and irritancy increase as pH decreases. Rosacea-prone users should use a BHA or PHA instead of glycolic acid. "The most researched AHA with the most proven skin benefits" (INCI Decoder).

**Photosensitivity:** Yes — AHAs thin the stratum corneum temporarily; always use SPF when using glycolics.
**Management:** start at 1–2× per week; avoid stacking with retinol in the same session.

---

### Lactic Acid (AHA)
*INCI Decoder classification: exfoliant, moisturiser/humectant / superstar*

Lactic acid has a larger molecular size than glycolic acid, which means shallower skin penetration and lower irritancy. Has both exfoliant and humectant properties — this dual action makes it a better entry-point AHA for beginners or sensitive skin. Same photosensitivity considerations as glycolic acid.

**Photosensitivity:** Yes — consistent SPF use required.
**Management:** gentler first AHA to introduce; can be used at higher frequency than glycolic for most skin types.

---

### Salicylic Acid (BHA)
*INCI Decoder classification: exfoliant, anti-acne / superstar*

"Gold standard ingredient for treating problem skin" (INCI Decoder). Exfoliates both the skin surface and inside pores (lipophilic — dissolves in sebum). Anti-inflammatory, making it more suitable for acne-prone skin than AHAs. Less irritating than glycolic acid for most users. Can cause dryness at higher concentrations or frequencies. For blackheads and comedonal acne it is more effective than benzoyl peroxide.

**Photosensitivity:** None established — but SPF is always recommended.
**Management:** moderate introduction pace; useful year-round; morning or evening use.

---

### Benzoyl Peroxide
*INCI Decoder note: "gold standard topical ingredient in treating acne" but pro-oxidant*

"The closest thing we have to a miracle cure for acne" (INCI Decoder) — highly effective against inflammatory acne caused by P. acnes bacteria. However, it is a **pro-oxidant** that generates reactive oxygen species (ROS), killing bacteria but also stressing surrounding skin cells. Long-term use at high concentrations accelerates photoaging. INCI Decoder recommends it "as a last resort" for topical treatment, and pairing with antioxidant serum (morning) when used.

**Key facts from INCI Decoder:**
- Equally effective at 2.5%, 5%, and 10% — use the lowest concentration (2.5%) to minimise side effects
- No bacterial resistance develops (unlike antibiotics)
- Compatible with retinoids (often combined in clinical settings)
- Bleaches fabrics and bedding — use white pillowcases
- Skin-drying — heavy moisturiser required

**Photosensitivity:** Mild — SPF recommended.
**Management:** start 2–3× per week; use at night; antioxidant serum in the morning.

---

### Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C / L-Ascorbic Acid)
*INCI Decoder classification: antioxidant, skin brightening / superstar*

Effective concentration range: 5–20%. "Potent Vit. C serums might cause a slight tingling on sensitive skin" (INCI Decoder). Most irritation is pH-related — low-pH formulations (required for stability below 3.5) cause the tingling. Vitamin C + E work synergistically; ferulic acid doubles the photoprotection effect and stabilises vitamin C. The ingredient is "extremely unstable and oxidizes very easily in presence of light or air" — dark glass packaging matters.

**Photosensitivity:** None — actually provides additional UV photoprotection when layered under SPF.
**Management:** good tolerance for most skin types; sensitive users should start at lower concentrations (5–10%) or switch to more stable derivatives (AA2G, SAP).

---

### Niacinamide (Vitamin B3)
*INCI Decoder classification: cell-communicating ingredient, skin brightening / superstar*

"A multi-functional skincare superstar with several proven benefits" (INCI Decoder). Benefits at 4–5%: anti-aging, wrinkle smoothing, brown spot fading. Increases ceramide synthesis for a stronger barrier. Effective for acne, rosacea, and atopic dermatitis. **Exceptionally well tolerated** — the lowest irritancy potential of all actives in this KB. The historical concern about niacinamide + vitamin C producing niacin (skin flushing) is largely debunked at cosmetically relevant concentrations and temperatures.

**Photosensitivity:** None.
**Management:** can be used morning and evening; no slow-introduction required; compatible with most other actives.

---

### Sodium Hyaluronate (Hyaluronic Acid)
*INCI Decoder classification: skin-identical ingredient, moisturiser/humectant / goodie*
*Irritancy: 0/10 | Comedogenicity: 0/10 (INCI Decoder)*

A skin-identical humectant that can hold up to 1000× its weight in water. Zero irritancy and zero comedogenic risk confirmed by INCI Decoder scoring. Sodium hyaluronate (the salt form) and hyaluronic acid are functionally identical in formulation, with sodium hyaluronate being more stable and cheaper. High molecular weight HA sits on the skin surface; low molecular weight penetrates more shallowly.

**Photosensitivity:** None.
**Management:** no introduction protocol needed; safe for all skin types; use on damp skin for best humectant effect.

---

### Ceramides
*INCI Decoder classification: skin-identical ingredient / goodie*

Ceramides are lipids that form the mortar between skin cells in the stratum corneum. They are skin-identical — the skin produces its own ceramides. Zero irritancy, zero comedogenic risk. Essential for barrier repair after aggressive active use. Ceramide NP (the most common in cosmetics) directly reinforces barrier function and reduces transepidermal water loss (TEWL).

**Photosensitivity:** None.
**Management:** can be used at any time; particularly valuable during retinol or AHA introduction to buffer barrier disruption.

---

### Peptides
*INCI Decoder classification: cell-communicating ingredient / goodie*

Peptides (e.g. Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1) signal fibroblasts to produce collagen. Well tolerated with no established irritancy. Mechanism is different from retinol (which directly binds nuclear receptors) — peptides are the gentler alternative for users who cannot tolerate retinoids. Results appear over 3–6 months at effective concentrations.

**Photosensitivity:** None.
**Management:** compatible with all other actives; no special introduction protocol needed.

---

### Azelaic Acid
*INCI Decoder classification: anti-acne, soothing / superstar*

"Superstar ingredient with antibacterial, skin cell regulating, anti-inflammatory and skin-lightening magic properties" (INCI Decoder). Particularly effective for acne-prone or rosacea-prone skin. Available as OTC up to 10% in the EU; prescription-strength in the US. Very mild, transient tingling in the first 1–2 weeks of use typically resolves on its own.

**Photosensitivity:** None established — but SPF always recommended for users treating PIH.
**Management:** low-irritancy active suitable for sensitive and rosacea-prone users; gentler than AHAs for similar concerns.

---

### Zinc Oxide (SPF active)
*INCI Decoder classification: sunscreen / goodie*
*Irritancy: 0/10 | Comedogenicity: 1/10 (INCI Decoder)*

Broadest-spectrum UV filter available, covering UVB, UVA II, and UVA I. "Highly stable and non-irritating" — also used as a skin protectant and anti-irritant (diaper rash formulations). Comedogenicity score of 1/10 is low and clinically insignificant for most users. The primary drawback is white cast (especially in non-nanoparticle form). Nanoparticle zinc oxide is widely used and remains on the skin surface rather than absorbing into deeper layers.

---

### Titanium Dioxide (SPF active)
*INCI Decoder classification: sunscreen, colorant / goodie*

Provides UVB and UVA II coverage (narrower than zinc oxide). Zero irritancy. Also leaves a white cast, though slightly more pronounced than zinc oxide. Often combined with zinc oxide for full-spectrum coverage.
