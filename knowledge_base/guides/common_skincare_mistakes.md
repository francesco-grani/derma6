# Common Skincare Mistakes

## Overview

Most skincare failures — persistent breakouts, irritation, unexpected worsening, or simply seeing no results — stem from a predictable set of errors. These mistakes are especially common among beginners building their first routine. Understanding them prevents months of frustration.

## 1. Over-Exfoliating

**What it looks like:** using an AHA toner daily, a BHA serum daily, a physical scrub twice a week, and an exfoliating cleanser — all simultaneously.

**What happens:** the stratum corneum (protective barrier) is progressively stripped. The skin becomes red, inflamed, and reactive. The protective lipid matrix (ceramides, fatty acids, cholesterol) is disrupted. TEWL increases. Paradoxically, the skin may become more oily as sebaceous glands compensate for barrier disruption.

**Signs of over-exfoliation:** persistent tightness and shininess (but not sebum shine), redness, stinging when applying any product, increased sensitivity, acne-like bumps that don't clear.

**Fix:** stop all exfoliants for 1–2 weeks, use a gentle ceramide cleanser and a ceramide moisturiser only. Reintroduce one exfoliant, 2× per week maximum.

## 2. Skipping or Under-Applying SPF

**What it looks like:** skipping sunscreen on "cloudy days," "indoor days," applying only a fingertip amount, or relying on SPF-containing moisturiser applied in insufficient quantity.

**What happens:** UV damage accumulates daily. Retinol, vitamin C, and AHA results are partially reversed by unprotected UV exposure. Hyperpigmentation worsens. Premature ageing accelerates.

**Fix:** apply SPF 50 every morning as the last step — 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon for the face. Indoors or cloudy conditions still expose skin to UVA (which penetrates clouds and glass).

## 3. Introducing Too Many Actives Simultaneously

**What it looks like:** starting a new routine with retinol, vitamin C, AHA, niacinamide, and BHA all at once in week one.

**What happens:** if a reaction occurs, it is impossible to identify the culprit. The skin barrier is overwhelmed by multiple actives with different mechanisms and pH requirements. Most people underestimate how potent new actives can be.

**Fix:** introduce one new active at a time, with at least 2–4 weeks between introductions. Log how the skin responds. Only add the next product when the current one is well-tolerated.

## 4. Applying Retinol on Damp Skin

**What it looks like:** washing the face and immediately applying retinol while skin is still damp or wet.

**What happens:** damp skin has an altered barrier permeability — actives absorb faster and more deeply. For retinol, this significantly increases the amount that penetrates, which increases irritation. This is especially problematic for beginners with no retinol tolerance.

**Fix:** after cleansing, pat skin completely dry, wait 10–15 minutes, then apply retinol. Alternatively, apply a light moisturiser first (the "sandwich" or "buffer" method) to slow absorption.

## 5. Not Waiting for Hyaluronic Acid to Seal

**What it looks like:** applying an HA serum and then not following with a moisturiser, especially in dry or air-conditioned environments.

**What happens:** HA is a humectant — it draws moisture from wherever it can. In dry environments (<70% humidity), it draws moisture upward from the dermis, increasing TEWL and leaving the skin feeling drier than before.

**Fix:** always follow HA with a moisturiser (cream or lotion) to seal in the hydration HA has attracted.

## 6. Mixing Incompatible Actives in the Same Step

**Common incompatible pairs applied simultaneously:**

| Combination | Problem |
|---|---|
| Retinol + benzoyl peroxide | BPO oxidises and deactivates retinol |
| Vitamin C (L-AA) + retinol | Low pH of L-AA degrades retinol |
| Copper peptides + vitamin C | Ascorbic acid oxidises copper ions, reducing efficacy |
| AHA/BHA + retinol (same session) | Dramatically increases irritation; barrier damage risk |

**Fix:** see Routine Sequencing Rules. Use AM/PM separation for conflicting actives. Do not layer them directly.

## 7. Not Patch Testing New Products

**What it looks like:** applying a new concentrated serum across the entire face on day one.

**What happens:** if a contact allergy or sensitivity reaction occurs, it affects the entire face — a much harder recovery than a localised reaction. Some reactions take 24–48 hours to appear (Type IV delayed hypersensitivity).

**Fix:** apply a small amount of any new product to the inner forearm or jawline. Wait 24 hours before full application. Wait 48 hours for high-risk actives (acids, retinoids, benzoyl peroxide) on sensitive skin.

## 8. Treating Purging as a Reason to Quit Retinol

**What it looks like:** starting retinol, experiencing more breakouts in weeks 2–4, and stopping.

**What happens:** retinol accelerates cell turnover, pushing existing microcomedones (clogs forming under the surface) to the surface faster. This looks like a breakout but is actually congestion that was already there. Stopping at this point abandons the process just before it would have worked.

**Fix:** distinguish purging (located in areas that normally break out, starts within 2–4 weeks of starting retinol, resolves within 4–8 weeks) from a true reaction (new breakout areas, worsening persists beyond 8 weeks). Reduce frequency but do not stop.

## 9. Using Alcohol-Heavy Toners

**What it looks like:** using toners with denatured alcohol (alcohol denat.) listed in the first few ingredients.

**What happens:** alcohol rapidly evaporates water from the surface, gives a temporary "clean" feel, but destroys the skin barrier with regular use. Increases TEWL, strips ceramides, triggers compensatory sebum production.

**Fix:** switch to alcohol-free, hydrating toners (ingredients: water, glycerin, hyaluronic acid, niacinamide, centella asiatica).
