# Peptides Profile

## Overview

Peptides are short chains of amino acids (typically 2–50 amino acids) linked by peptide bonds. In skincare, they act as biological messengers that signal skin cells to perform specific functions, most notably collagen synthesis and structural repair. Unlike retinol, peptides achieve anti-aging effects without causing irritation or photosensitivity, making them suitable for sensitive skin and daytime use. 

**Paula's Choice rating:** Best — benefits: Anti-Aging, Hydration, Soothing  
**INCI Decoder classification:** superstar — cell-communicating ingredient

## Classification of Skincare Peptides

### Signal Peptides (Matrikines)
These peptides mimic fragments of the extracellular matrix (ECM) that are released when collagen breaks down — essentially signalling to fibroblasts that repair is needed. This tricks the skin into upregulating collagen, elastin, and other ECM proteins.

Key examples:
- **Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4 (Matrixyl):** the most studied signal peptide. Stimulates production of collagen I, III, and IV, and fibronectin. Clinical studies show reduction in wrinkle depth with sustained use (Schagen, 2017).
- **Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 and Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7 (Matrixyl 3000):** synergistic combination; promotes collagen and hyaluronic acid synthesis. Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 has been shown to reduce deep wrinkles by 39.4% and improve skin tone by 15.5% (INCI Decoder).

The palmitoyl prefix indicates fatty acid modification — this improves lipid solubility and skin penetration of otherwise hydrophilic peptides.

### Carrier Peptides
These peptides chelate (bind and transport) trace minerals — most importantly copper — into the skin, where they support enzymatic processes for wound healing and collagen synthesis.

Key example:
- **Copper Peptide GHK-Cu (glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine:Cu²⁺):** promotes collagen and glycosaminoglycan synthesis; has antioxidant and wound-healing properties. Notably, copper peptides can cause a blue-green tint in high concentrations (Pickart & Margolina, 2018).

### Neurotransmitter-Inhibiting Peptides
These peptides reduce the depth of expression lines by temporarily inhibiting acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction, relaxing facial muscles. Often described as "topical Botox-like" — although the effect is milder and superficial.

Key example:
- **Acetyl Hexapeptide-3/8 (Argireline):** widely used; clinical evidence shows modest reduction in forehead line depth with sustained twice-daily use (Gorouhi & Maibach, 2009).

### Enzyme-Inhibiting Peptides
Block enzymes that break down skin matrix proteins.
- **Leuphasyl, Trylagen:** inhibit serine proteases that degrade collagen and elastin.

## Benefits

- Stimulate collagen and elastin production (signal peptides).
- Improve skin firmness and reduce fine lines over 8–12 weeks of use.
- Support wound healing and barrier recovery (copper peptides).
- Reduce expression line depth (neurotransmitter peptides).
- Well tolerated — minimal risk of irritation across skin types.

## How to Use

- Apply as a serum before moisturiser.
- Can be used AM, PM, or both.
- Allow peptide serums to absorb before applying heavier products.
- No pH requirements — apply after any low-pH actives have absorbed.

## Conflicts and Cautions

- **Copper peptides + vitamin C:** ascorbic acid can oxidise copper ions, potentially reducing GHK-Cu efficacy. Use copper peptides in PM, vitamin C in AM.
- **Copper peptides + AHAs/BHAs:** low pH can destabilise copper peptides. Apply them in separate routines or allow acid to absorb and pH to rise before applying.
- **Peptides + AHA/BHA in the same step:** while not harmful, the acidic environment can disrupt peptide stability. Apply peptide serum after acid has absorbed.
- Peptides are generally safe with retinol, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, and ceramides.

## Stability Considerations

Peptides degrade in the presence of high heat, direct sunlight, and highly acidic or alkaline environments. Store products in a cool, dark location. Some peptides (especially copper) are best formulated in opaque packaging.
