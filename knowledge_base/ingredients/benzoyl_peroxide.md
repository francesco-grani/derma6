# Benzoyl Peroxide Profile

## Overview

Benzoyl peroxide (BPO) is an organic peroxide and one of the most effective OTC treatments for acne. Unlike salicylic acid, which primarily unclogs pores, BPO works as a potent **bactericidal agent** targeting Cutibacterium acnes (C. acnes), the bacteria most responsible for acne lesion formation. It also has mild keratolytic (exfoliating) and comedolytic effects. BPO has decades of clinical evidence and is recommended by major dermatology bodies worldwide.

**Paula's Choice rating:** Best — benefits: Anti-Acne, Smooths Bumpy Skin  
**INCI Decoder classification:** superstar — antibacterial

## Mechanism of Action

BPO releases free oxygen radicals when it contacts the skin, creating a highly oxidising environment within the follicle and on the skin surface. This oxidative burst is bactericidal — it kills C. acnes by oxidising its bacterial proteins and cell membranes. Key properties:

1. **Antibacterial (bactericidal, not bacteriostatic):** kills C. acnes rapidly and completely rather than merely inhibiting growth. This is why, unlike antibiotics, BPO does not generate antibiotic-resistant C. acnes strains — the oxidative mechanism is not susceptible to mutation-based resistance.
2. **Comedolytic:** mildly normalises follicular keratinisation, helping to clear comedones.
3. **Anti-inflammatory:** reduces sebaceous gland lipid production and decreases inflammatory cytokines in the follicle.

## Concentrations

| Concentration | Notes |
|---|---|
| 2.5% | Equally effective as higher concentrations for C. acnes killing; significantly less irritating |
| 5% | Balanced potency and tolerability; most common OTC strength |
| 10% | More irritating; no greater bactericidal effect vs 2.5–5%; not recommended for routine use |

**Key finding from research:** 2.5% BPO is as effective at reducing C. acnes counts as 10% BPO, but causes significantly less dryness, irritation, and peeling. Starting with the lowest effective concentration is best practice.

## Formulations

- **Leave-on treatments (gels, lotions, creams):** provide sustained exposure; more effective for clearing acne.
- **Wash-off cleansers:** lower residual concentration but well-tolerated; useful for body acne and sensitive facial skin.
- PM application is preferred for leave-on products — allows maximum contact time without sun interaction.

## Benefits

- Highly effective against inflammatory acne (papules, pustules, cysts).
- Effective against comedonal acne.
- No risk of bacterial resistance — important advantage over antibiotic treatments.
- Often used in combination with topical antibiotics (clindamycin) in prescription formulations to prevent resistance.
- Can produce visible improvement within 2–4 weeks.

## Fabric Bleaching

**Critical warning:** BPO bleaches fabric, hair, and coloured materials on contact. It will permanently discolour pillow cases, towels, and clothing. Use white or dedicated pillow covers. Wash hands thoroughly after applying. Apply BPO before getting into bed, not immediately before changing clothes.

## Conflicts and Cautions

- **Do NOT combine with retinol:** BPO oxidises retinol molecules, chemically deactivating them. If both are needed, use BPO AM and retinol PM, never layered together.
- **Do NOT combine with vitamin C:** BPO oxidises L-ascorbic acid, rendering it inactive. Same AM/PM separation applies.
- **Avoid with tretinoin (prescription retinoid):** same inactivation concern.
- **Dryness and irritation:** BPO frequently causes dryness, flaking, and redness, especially at higher concentrations or on sensitive skin. Counteract with a ceramide-rich, non-comedogenic moisturiser.
- **Avoid contact with eyes, mouth, and nostrils.**
- **Sun sensitivity:** BPO can increase UV sensitivity; daily SPF recommended.
- **Initial over-drying:** start with 2–3× per week, then increase frequency as tolerance builds.
