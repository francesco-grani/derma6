# Azelaic Acid Profile

## Overview

Azelaic acid (AzA) is a naturally occurring dicarboxylic acid found in wheat, rye, and barley, and produced by Malassezia yeast on human skin. It is a versatile multi-action ingredient with antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, comedolytic, and skin-brightening properties. Prescription formulations (15–20%) are used for rosacea and acne; OTC formulations (10%) are available in many markets. It is notable for being one of the few actives considered safe during pregnancy. 

**Paula's Choice rating:** Best — benefits: Anti-Acne, Dark Spot Fading, Smooths Bumpy Skin.  
**INCI Decoder classification:** superstar — anti-acne, soothing, buffering.

## Mechanism of Action

Azelaic acid works through several simultaneous pathways:

### Antimicrobial
AzA inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by targeting specific enzymes in Cutibacterium acnes and Staphylococcus epidermidis. This action is bacteriostatic (inhibitory) rather than bactericidal. Unlike antibiotics, AzA does not generate resistant strains.

### Anti-inflammatory
AzA scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated by neutrophils during inflammatory acne lesion formation. It also inhibits the kallikrein-5 serine protease pathway, which drives the redness, flushing, and papule formation in rosacea.

### Tyrosinase Inhibition (Skin Brightening)
AzA competitively inhibits tyrosinase — the key enzyme in melanin biosynthesis — more selectively than kojic acid and without the toxicity of hydroquinone. This makes it effective for:
- Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH).
- Melasma.
- General uneven skin tone.

Unlike some brightening actives, AzA selectively targets hyperactive melanocytes, making it less likely to depigment normally pigmented skin.

### Comedolytic Action
AzA normalises the abnormal keratinisation within follicles that leads to comedone formation, acting similarly to retinoids but with far less irritation.

## Concentrations

| Concentration | Availability | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | OTC (most markets) | Mild acne, PIH, general brightening |
| 15% | Prescription (gel, foam) | Rosacea, moderate acne |
| 20% | Prescription (cream) | Rosacea, moderate-severe acne, melasma |

## Benefits

- Dual-action for acne: antimicrobial + comedolytic.
- Reduces rosacea flushing, papules, and pustules — one of few OTC options with evidence for rosacea.
- Fades PIH and melasma effectively.
- Safe for darker skin tones (Fitzpatrick III–VI) — minimal risk of post-procedure hyperpigmentation.
- Safe in pregnancy — categorised as Pregnancy Category B.
- Does not cause sun sensitivity or require SPF as strictly as AHAs (though SPF is always recommended).

## How to Use

- Apply as a serum or cream to affected areas, once or twice daily.
- Can be used AM or PM.
- Initial tingling or stinging is common — this usually subsides after the first 2–4 weeks as the skin acclimates.
- Results for brightening take 8–12 weeks; acne improvement can begin within 4 weeks.

## Compatibility with Other Actives

AzA is one of the most compatible actives in skincare:
- Safe with retinol, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, ceramides.
- Safe with AHAs and BHAs (though not necessary to layer; use one exfoliant at a time).
- Safe with vitamin C — they work synergistically on PIH and melanin.
- Safe with benzoyl peroxide — complementary mechanisms for acne.
- Slight potential for interaction with other strong acids (AHA/BHA) at high concentrations; space them out in routine.

## Cautions

- Initial stinging and tingling is expected, not a sign of allergy.
- Contact dermatitis (true allergy) is rare but possible; patch test if unsure.
- Hypopigmentation risk in very high concentrations on normally pigmented skin — use formulated products at specified concentrations.
