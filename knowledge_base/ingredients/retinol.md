# Retinol Profile

## Overview

Retinol is a vitamin A derivative and one of the most extensively researched anti-ageing and acne-fighting ingredients in skincare. It belongs to the retinoid family, which includes over-the-counter forms (retinol, retinaldehyde, retinyl esters) and prescription forms (tretinoin, adapalene, tazarotene).

## Mechanism of Action

Retinol itself is biologically inactive. After topical application it undergoes a two-step enzymatic conversion in the skin: retinol → retinaldehyde → retinoic acid (all-trans retinoic acid, or ATRA). Retinoic acid binds to nuclear retinoic acid receptors (RARα, RARβ, RARγ) and retinoid X receptors (RXRs), which act as transcription factors. This binding upregulates genes involved in:

- **Keratinocyte differentiation and cell turnover:** faster replacement of dull, surface cells.
- **Collagen synthesis:** stimulates fibroblasts to produce types I and III collagen; simultaneously inhibits collagenase (MMP-1), reducing collagen breakdown.
- **Comedone resolution:** normalises follicular keratinisation, preventing pore clogging.
- **Melanin regulation:** reduces tyrosinase activity and disperses existing melanin granules, helping fade post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.

Because the conversion to retinoic acid is rate-limited, OTC retinol is less irritating than prescription tretinoin but requires more time to show results.

## Concentrations

| Concentration | Use Case |
|---|---|
| 0.025–0.05% | Beginners, sensitive skin |
| 0.1–0.3% | Intermediate — after 8–12 weeks of tolerance building |
| 0.5–1.0% | Experienced users; maximum OTC strength in most markets |
| >1% | Prescription territory (tretinoin 0.025–0.1%) |

## How to Use

- **Time of day:** PM only. Retinol degrades under UV light and can increase photosensitivity.
- **Starting frequency:** 2× per week for 4–6 weeks, then increase gradually to nightly if tolerated.
- **Application:** apply to dry skin (waiting 10–15 minutes after cleansing reduces irritation on sensitive skin).
- **SPF is mandatory** every morning when using retinol — skin becomes more vulnerable to UV damage.

## Benefits

- Reduces fine lines and wrinkles (well-documented in peer-reviewed literature).
- Accelerates cell turnover → smoother texture and more even tone.
- Clears and prevents comedonal and inflammatory acne.
- Fades post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) over 8–24 weeks.

## Cautions and Conflicts

- **Purging:** an initial breakout during weeks 2–6 is normal as cell turnover accelerates.
- **Irritation (retinoid dermatitis):** redness, peeling, dryness — manage with a gentle moisturiser.
- **Do not combine in the same session:** AHAs/BHAs (stacking acids with retinol dramatically increases irritation), benzoyl peroxide (oxidises and deactivates retinol).
- **Pregnancy:** all retinoids are contraindicated during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- **Buffering technique:** applying moisturiser before retinol (sandwich method) reduces irritation for beginners.
