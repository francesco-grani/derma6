# SPF Actives Guide

## Overview

Sunscreen (SPF) is the single most evidence-backed skincare product for preventing photoageing, hyperpigmentation, and skin cancer. Daily application is not optional — it is the foundation of any skincare routine. Without SPF, the benefits of actives like vitamin C, retinol, and AHAs are partially undermined because UV exposure re-damages the skin these actives are working to repair. 

**Paula's Choice rating:** Best — benefits: Anti-Aging, Anti-Acne  
**INCI Decoder classification:** superstar — cell-communicating ingredient

## UV Radiation Types

| UV Type | Wavelength | Penetration | Primary Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| UVA (320–400 nm) | Long | Penetrates glass, clouds, deep into dermis | Photoageing (collagen breakdown), tanning, melanoma risk |
| UVB (290–320 nm) | Medium | Reaches epidermis | Sunburn, DNA damage, squamous/basal cell carcinoma |
| UVC (100–290 nm) | Short | Blocked by ozone layer | Does not reach Earth's surface |

**UVA ages; UVB burns.** Broad-spectrum protection against both is required.

## How SPF Protects the Skin from UV

SPF protects the skin by forming a filter layer that stops UV radiation from reaching living skin cells. Mineral filters **reflect and scatter** UV away from the skin, while chemical filters **absorb** UV photons and convert them into harmless heat. By intercepting UV before it penetrates, SPF blocks both damage pathways: **UVB** causes sunburn and direct DNA damage in the epidermis, and **UVA** penetrates deeper to break down collagen and drive photoageing, pigmentation, and melanoma risk. Blocking this UV energy prevents the DNA-damage and free-radical cascade that would otherwise cause mutations, collagen loss, and dark spots. This is why broad-spectrum SPF 50+ is applied daily — it filters both UVA and UVB, blocking 98–99% of UVB.

## Filter Types

### Mineral (Physical) Filters
Inorganic compounds that primarily **reflect and scatter** UV radiation (though they also absorb UV):
- **Zinc oxide (ZnO):** provides broad-spectrum UVA + UVB coverage; photostable; gentle on sensitive skin; white cast at higher concentrations (nanoparticle forms reduce cast). Zinc oxide is considered to offer the broadest range of UV protection, covering UVB, UVA II, and UVA I almost uniformly (INCI Decoder). It is also recognized as a skin protectant and anti-irritant, often used to treat skin irritations such as diaper rash.
- **Titanium dioxide (TiO2):** primarily UVB protection; often combined with zinc oxide for complete coverage; more prone to white cast. Titanium dioxide provides good protection primarily in the UVB and UVA II range but is less effective at UVA I (INCI Decoder). 

Mineral sunscreens are recommended for sensitive, rosacea-prone, and acne-prone skin due to lower irritation risk.

### Chemical (Organic) Filters
Carbon-containing molecules that **absorb** UV photons and convert them to heat:
- **Avobenzone (Butyl Methoxydibenzoylmethane):** UVA filter; inherently photounstable — degrades in sunlight unless stabilised with octocrylene or bemotrizinol. Common in US formulations.
- **Tinosorb S (Bis-Ethylhexyloxyphenol Methoxyphenyl Triazine):** broad-spectrum UVA + UVB; highly photostable; widely used in EU, Australia. Not yet approved by FDA.
- **Tinosorb M:** hybrid filter (acts as both mineral and chemical filter); broad-spectrum, photostable.
- **Mexoryl SX and XL:** UVA filters; photostable; common in EU formulations.
- **Uvinul A Plus, Uvinul T 150:** newer photostable UV filters common in European and Asian formulations.

European and Asian sunscreen formulations generally provide superior UVA protection compared to US-market formulations due to access to newer, photostable chemical filters not yet FDA-approved.

## SPF and PA Ratings

| Rating System | What It Measures | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| SPF (Sun Protection Factor) | UVB protection only | SPF 30 blocks ~97%; SPF 50 blocks ~98%; SPF 50+ blocks 98–99% |
| PA (Protection Grade of UVA) | UVA protection (Japanese/Asian system) | PA+ to PA++++ |
| PPD (Persistent Pigment Darkening) | UVA protection (EU system) | Must be ≥ SPF/3 in EU |
| Broad-spectrum label (US) | Passes FDA UVA test | Minimum standard |

**Minimum recommended:** SPF 50 / PA+++ (European Dermatology Forum recommendation). SPF 30 is the absolute minimum for any sun exposure.

## Application Requirements

- **Amount:** 2 mg/cm² is the standard test application. For the face, this is approximately 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon (1.25–2.5 ml). Most people apply only 25–50% of this amount, achieving SPF far below the rated value.
- **Timing:** Apply as the last step in the AM routine, after all other skincare products (moisturiser, vitamin C, etc.). Allow 1–2 minutes for skin to settle before sunscreen application.
- **Reapplication:** reapply every 2 hours during direct sun exposure. On a typical indoor day with commuting, one morning application is generally adequate.
- **Order:** SPF is always the last skincare step. Applying anything on top (tinted moisturiser excepted) can disrupt the SPF film.

## SPF and Skincare Actives

- **Retinol:** increases photosensitivity — SPF is essential.
- **AHAs:** increase UV sensitivity — SPF is essential.
- **Vitamin C:** provides additional photoprotection (antioxidant) but does not replace SPF.
- **BHA (salicylic acid):** mild photosensitisation; SPF recommended.
