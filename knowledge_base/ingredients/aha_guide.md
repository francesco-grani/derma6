# AHA Guide

## Overview

Alpha-hydroxy acids (AHAs) are water-soluble chemical exfoliants derived from natural sources (fruit acids, milk, sugar cane). Unlike physical scrubs, AHAs dissolve the bonds between dead skin cells without mechanical abrasion, delivering controlled, even exfoliation. They are among the most evidence-backed actives for improving skin texture, tone, and mild acne.

## Types of AHAs

### Glycolic Acid
Derived from sugar cane. Smallest molecular weight (~76 Da) of all AHAs, which gives it the deepest skin penetration and the strongest efficacy — but also the greatest irritation potential. Most studied AHA in clinical literature. Effective range: 5–15% for leave-on products (up to 30–70% for professional peels).

### Lactic Acid
Derived from milk. Larger molecule than glycolic acid, penetrates more slowly — gentler and better tolerated for sensitive skin. Added benefit: acts as a humectant, attracting moisture. Effective range: 5–12%. Also has mild skin-lightening properties.

### Mandelic Acid
Derived from bitter almonds. Largest common AHA molecule — slowest penetration, most tolerable, suitable for sensitive and darker skin tones (lower risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation from over-exfoliation). Effective range: 5–15%. Has mild antibacterial properties, useful for acne.

### Other AHAs
- **Malic acid (apples):** typically used in combination formulas.
- **Citric acid:** more commonly used as a pH adjuster than a primary exfoliant.
- **Tartaric acid (grapes):** minor exfoliant role.

## Mechanism of Action

AHAs are hydroxy acids with a hydroxyl group on the alpha carbon. At an effective pH (<4, optimally 3–3.5), they penetrate the stratum corneum and disrupt calcium-dependent cell-to-cell adhesion molecules called **desmosomes**. This loosens the cohesion between corneocytes (dead skin cells), allowing them to shed — a process called **corneodesmolysis**. The result is accelerated skin cell turnover without physical friction.

At higher concentrations (professional peels), AHAs also stimulate new collagen production in the dermis by triggering wound-healing signalling pathways.

## Benefits

- Smoother skin texture by removing built-up dead cells.
- More even skin tone and reduced hyperpigmentation (PIH, sun spots).
- Improved absorption of subsequent skincare products.
- Mild comedolytic effect for superficial acne.
- Glycolic acid at >8% has documented collagen-stimulating effects with long-term use.

## pH Requirement

Effective AHA products must have a pH below 4 (ideally 3–3.5). At higher pH, the acids exist predominantly in ionised form and cannot penetrate the stratum corneum. Always check product pH or use products from reputable brands who formulate correctly.

## Photosensitising Effect

AHAs increase UV sensitivity by thinning the stratum corneum. Studies have shown a measurable increase in UVB sensitivity with continued use. **Daily SPF is mandatory** when using AHAs. This is not optional.

## How to Use

- Apply to clean, dry skin, after cleansing.
- Leave-on products: use 2–3× per week initially; daily use is possible after tolerance is established.
- Wait 10–20 minutes after applying an acid before applying other products, to allow the low pH to work and then buffer slightly before layering.
- Use PM to avoid immediate UV exposure after application.

## Conflicts and Cautions

- **Do not combine with retinol in the same session:** stacking acid exfoliation with retinol dramatically increases irritation and barrier damage risk. Alternate nights, or use one in AM and one in PM with a longer buffer.
- **Do not layer AHA + BHA together carelessly:** combining two exfoliating acids can over-exfoliate the skin. Unless a product is specifically formulated as a combined acid, use one at a time.
- **Avoid on broken, inflamed, or severely compromised skin.**
- **Pregnancy:** glycolic acid at low concentrations is generally considered safe; higher concentrations should be discussed with a physician.
