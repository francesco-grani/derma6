## BHA Guide

## Overview

Beta-hydroxy acid (BHA) refers primarily to **salicylic acid** in the context of skincare (beta-hydroxybutanoic acid is the other BHA but has negligible cosmetic use). Salicylic acid is derived naturally from willow bark (Salix species) and is the gold-standard OTC ingredient for acne and oily skin management. Its oil solubility — the key distinction from AHAs — allows it to penetrate into pore linings where AHAs cannot reach. 

**Paula's Choice rating:** Best — benefits: Anti-Acne, Blackhead Reducing, Soothing.  
**INCI Decoder classification:** superstar — exfoliant, anti-acne, soothing.

## Mechanism of Action

### Keratolytic Action
Salicylic acid softens and loosens the keratin structure within follicles by disrupting the hydrogen bonds that hold corneocytes together. Unlike AHAs, which work on the surface of the stratum corneum, salicylic acid penetrates the **pilosebaceous unit** (the hair follicle + sebaceous gland complex), dissolving sebaceous plugs, blackheads (open comedones), and whiteheads (closed comedones) from within. Salicylic acid is recognized as an over-the-counter topical acne product by the U.S. FDA, when used in concentrations of 0.5-2% (⚠ source conflict — verify).

### Anti-inflammatory Action
Salicylic acid inhibits the prostaglandin and leukotriene synthesis pathways (similar mechanism to aspirin, its chemical cousin). This makes it useful not just for clearing existing acne but for reducing the redness and swelling of inflammatory lesions. It is a potent anti-inflammatory agent, making it effective for treating both acne and other skin irritations.

### Antibacterial Contribution
Salicylic acid lowers the pH of the follicular environment, creating an inhospitable condition for Cutibacterium acnes (formerly Propionibacterium acnes), the bacterium most associated with acne lesion formation.

## Concentrations

| Concentration | Use |
|---|---|
| 0.5% | Sensitive skin; introductory dose |
| 1% | Most daily-use leave-on products (serums, toners) |
| 2% | Maximum OTC concentration; wash-off cleansers, targeted treatments |
| >2% | Prescription-only or professional peels (up to 50%) |

Higher concentrations do not linearly increase efficacy but do increase irritation. At 2%, salicylic acid is fully effective for OTC acne management. For more stubborn concerns, higher concentrations can make a dramatic difference (⚠ source conflict — verify).

## pH Requirement

Optimal efficacy occurs at pH 3–4. At higher pH, the acid is ionised and cannot penetrate lipid-rich pore walls. However, salicylic acid is somewhat more forgiving than glycolic acid in this regard due to its lipid solubility. It works most effectively in leave-on formulas with a pH range between 3-4.

## Benefits

- Unclogs pores and prevents new comedones (blackheads, whiteheads).
- Reduces inflammatory acne lesions (papules, pustules).
- Controls excess sebum on the skin surface.
- Smooths skin texture with regular use.
- Effective for back and chest acne (body washes at 2%).
- Helps reverse the appearance of dull, uneven skin tone, as well as other visible signs of aging skin.

## Photosensitivity vs AHAs

Salicylic acid does cause some increase in UV sensitivity, but the effect is significantly smaller than with glycolic or lactic acid, because BHA primarily works inside the pore rather than thinning the surface stratum corneum broadly. However, daily SPF is still recommended as best practice.

## How to Use

- Apply to clean, dry skin after cleansing.
- Leave-on (toner/serum): start 2–3× per week, increase to daily if tolerated.
- Wash-off (cleanser): use daily; contact time is short, so well tolerated.
- Can be used AM or PM; PM preferred for leave-on treatments to allow maximum contact time without immediate UV exposure.

## Conflicts and Cautions

- **Aspirin allergy:** patients with aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) hypersensitivity should use caution, though topical cross-reactivity is rare.
- **Do not combine with AHA in the same step** without caution — dual acid exfoliation can over-exfoliate. Formulated combination products exist and are generally safe, but ad-hoc layering of concentrated acid serums is risky.
- **Do not combine with retinol in the same session** — the combined exfoliation and retinoid cell turnover can cause severe barrier disruption.
- **Pregnancy:** salicylic acid at leave-on concentrations >2% is generally avoided; low-concentration wash-off products are considered low-risk but discuss with a physician.
- **Bleaching fabrics:** unlike benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid does not bleach fabric.
