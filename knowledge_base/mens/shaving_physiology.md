# Shaving Physiology

## Overview

Shaving is a daily mechanical process that directly affects the skin barrier, hair follicle health, and the overall condition of facial skin. Understanding the physiology of shaving helps explain why certain techniques and post-shave products are necessary, not optional. For men who shave regularly, shaving-related skin stress is a larger contributor to skin concerns than most topical actives.

## What Shaving Does to the Skin

### Stratum Corneum Removal

Each shaving pass removes approximately **1–2 cell layers of the stratum corneum** (the outermost protective layer of the epidermis). The stratum corneum is 10–20 layers thick under normal conditions; shaving thus removes a meaningful percentage of the barrier. With multiple passes (as in a traditional three-pass wet shave), cumulative removal is significant.

This barrier disruption:
- Increases trans-epidermal water loss (TEWL) temporarily.
- Increases skin permeability to allergens, irritants, and actives.
- Triggers a low-grade inflammatory response (redness, heat, sensitivity).

In the hours following shaving, the skin is in a temporarily compromised state. This is why post-shave application of harsh actives causes intense irritation.

### Mechanical Forces on Hair Follicles

A razor blade does not simply cut hair at the surface. The blade stretches the hair upward before cutting it (especially multi-blade razors, which are designed to cut the hair and allow it to retract below the skin surface). This creates an extremely close shave but also:
- Predisposes to ingrown hairs when curly hair curls back into the follicle.
- Can traumatise the follicle opening, contributing to follicular keratinisation.
- In users with coarse or curly hair, promotes pseudofolliculitis barbae (razor bumps).

## Wet Shaving vs Dry Shaving

### Wet Shaving (razor + water + foam/gel)

**Advantages:**
- Water softens the hair shaft, reducing cutting force by up to 70% (wet hair is significantly weaker than dry hair).
- Shaving cream/gel lubricates the skin surface, reducing friction coefficient between blade and skin.
- Pre-shave hydration of the beard (2 minutes of warm water or shaving immediately after showering) is the single most effective way to reduce razor burn.
- Closer shave achievable with less passes.

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires more preparation time.
- Risk of nicks and cuts, especially with straight or safety razors.

### Dry Shaving (electric razor, no water or foam)

**Advantages:**
- Faster, more convenient.
- Generally less traumatic to the stratum corneum per session.
- Reduced risk of ingrown hairs in most cases.

**Disadvantages:**
- Less close shave — the blade does not get as close to the follicle.
- Over time, electric rotary or foil razors can still cause cumulative barrier disruption, especially if blades are not regularly cleaned and replaced.
- Dry contact of electric razor head against un-lubricated skin can cause friction-induced redness.

**Net effect:** for skin health, electric shaving is generally less traumatic. For those prioritising closeness of shave, a well-executed wet shave with sharp blades and proper lubrication can achieve excellent results with comparable barrier impact.

## Shaving Direction and Hair Grain

Hair grows in a specific direction (the "grain") that varies across the face and neck.

| Pass Direction | Closeness | Irritation Risk |
|---|---|---|
| With the grain (WTG) | Moderate | Low |
| Across the grain (XTG) | Close | Moderate |
| Against the grain (ATG) | Very close | High |

**Recommended approach for irritation-prone skin:**
- First pass: always WTG — removes most hair with minimal trauma.
- If needed, second pass: XTG — significantly closer with limited additional irritation.
- ATG pass: only for those with no skin irritation concerns; significantly increases ingrown hair risk in curly-hair individuals.

## Multi-Blade vs Single-Blade Razors

### Multi-Blade Razors (3–5 blades)
Modern cartridge razors are designed so each successive blade cuts slightly closer than the previous one. The first blade lifts and stretches the hair; subsequent blades cut it below the surface. This achieves a very close shave in one pass but:
- Greater stratum corneum disruption per pass.
- Higher risk of ingrown hairs (hair cut below the skin surface can re-enter follicle).

### Single-Blade Razors (safety razors, straight razors)
One blade cuts at the skin surface without the hysteresis effect. Requires more skill and technique, but:
- Less stratum corneum disruption.
- Significantly lower ingrown hair risk.
- Less expensive long-term.
- More environmentally sustainable.

## Beard Softening Before Shaving

The keratin protein in hair is significantly weakened by water absorption. Hair soaked in warm water for 2 minutes becomes approximately 25–30% easier to cut. Practical approaches:
- Shave after a shower (2+ minutes of facial exposure to steam and warm water).
- Apply warm, damp towel to the face for 2 minutes before shaving.
- Wash the face with warm water and allow 2 minutes before applying shaving product.

A pre-shave oil (applied before shaving cream) provides an additional lubrication layer and further softens hair; beneficial for coarse beards.

## Male Skin Characteristics Relevant to Shaving

- Male facial skin is approximately **20–25% thicker** than female facial skin, due to higher androgen levels that stimulate the dermis.
- Male skin has a higher density of sebaceous glands on the face, producing more sebum — which can clog razor blades faster.
- Male skin has a slightly lower pH than female skin.
- Despite thicker skin, shaving creates a chronically disrupted barrier in the shaved zones that requires consistent post-shave care.
