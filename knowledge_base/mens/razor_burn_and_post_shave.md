# Razor Burn and Post-Shave Barrier Repair

## Overview

Razor burn is one of the most common skin complaints in men who shave. It is a form of acute mechanical irritation combined with barrier disruption, and if managed poorly (or ignored), it can lead to chronic inflammation, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH), ingrown hairs (pseudofolliculitis barbae), and increased skin sensitivity over time.

## What Causes Razor Burn

Razor burn occurs when the razor blade:
1. **Removes superficial skin cells:** shaving removes approximately 2 layers of the stratum corneum per pass, creating a temporarily compromised barrier.
2. **Creates friction-induced inflammation:** insufficient lubrication (inadequate shaving foam, dry shaving) amplifies mechanical trauma.
3. **Disrupts the hair follicle opening:** improper technique can cut hairs unevenly or at the wrong angle, predisposing to ingrown hairs.
4. **Activates inflammatory cascade:** mast cells in the disrupted skin release histamine and pro-inflammatory cytokines, causing the characteristic redness, swelling, and burning sensation.

### Razor Burn vs Razor Bumps

| | Razor Burn | Razor Bumps (Pseudofolliculitis barbae) |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Diffuse redness, irritation | Small red bumps, ingrown hairs |
| Cause | Friction + barrier disruption | Curly/coarse hairs re-entering follicle |
| Risk factors | Dry shaving, dull blade, sensitive skin | Coarse or curly hair, close shaving |
| Typical duration | 24–48 hours | Can persist; chronic if not managed |

Pseudofolliculitis barbae (PFB) is particularly common in men of African and Middle Eastern descent due to the naturally curly hair follicle shape.

## Symptoms

- Diffuse redness across the shaved area.
- Burning, stinging, or itching sensation immediately post-shave.
- Tender skin that is reactive to touch.
- Small red papules (in more severe cases or PFB).
- Hyperpigmentation in chronic cases (post-inflammatory dark spots).

## Immediate Treatment (After Razor Burn Occurs)

1. **Cool water rinse:** cool (not cold) water constricts blood vessels and reduces acute inflammation. Splash the face gently for 30–60 seconds.
2. **Pat dry gently:** use a soft, clean towel. Do not rub.
3. **No actives:** do not apply retinol, AHAs, BHAs, vitamin C, or benzoyl peroxide on razor-burned skin. The barrier is already compromised — these actives will absorb too rapidly and cause severe irritation and potential sensitisation.
4. **Apply a barrier repair moisturiser:** look for formulations with ceramides and no fragrance. Examples: CeraVe Moisturising Cream, Vanicream Moisturising Skin Cream, La Roche-Posay Toleriane.
5. **Niacinamide 4–5%:** if tolerated, niacinamide can help reduce redness and support barrier recovery on the following day.
6. **Aloe vera (pure):** can provide soothing relief; anti-inflammatory properties of alacetin and aloin reduce redness.

## Prevention Strategies

### Pre-Shave Preparation
- **Hydrate the beard:** wet the face with warm water for at least 2 minutes before shaving (or shave after showering). Warm water softens the hair shaft, reducing the force required to cut it by up to 70%.
- **Apply a quality shaving cream or gel:** ensures adequate lubrication. Avoid products with high alcohol content.

### During Shaving
- **Use a sharp blade:** a dull blade drags rather than cuts, multiplying friction. Change blades every 5–7 shaves or when any dragging is felt.
- **Shave with the grain (WTG) first:** the direction hair grows naturally. This is the least irritating pass. If a closer shave is needed, follow with an across-the-grain (ATG) pass; an against-the-grain (AGG) pass is the closest but most irritating.
- **No pressure:** the weight of the razor should do the work. Pressing harder drags the blade across the skin rather than cutting hair.
- **Rinse blade frequently:** between strokes, rinse in warm water to clear clogged hair and foam.

### Post-Shave Care
- **Alcohol-free aftershave balm:** alcohol-containing aftershaves provide a brief antiseptic benefit but cause significant drying and irritation. Replace with an alcohol-free balm or moisturiser.
- **Barrier moisturiser:** apply immediately after shaving while skin is still slightly damp.
- **SPF in the morning:** freshly shaved skin is more vulnerable to UV damage.

## For Ingrown Hairs (PFB)
- Exfoliate the beard area 2–3× per week with a mild BHA or AHA product (when skin is not acutely irritated).
- Single-blade razors and electric rotary shavers cause fewer ingrowns than multi-blade razors.
- Retinol on the beard area (3–4 nights per week) can reduce follicular keratinisation that contributes to ingrowns over time.
